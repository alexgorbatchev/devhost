# Read service logs in the page: proposed experience

Diagrams: [current](../log-minimap-current.html) (frozen at `edb5c0fc`), [proposed](../log-minimap-proposed.html). [Wireframe](../wireframes/log-minimap.html).

Status: design proposal, not implemented behavior. The [shared UX pattern reference](../patterns.md) wins where this proposal disagrees with it.

Source revision: alexgorbatchev/devhost `190a8bb53e8cd903d2db0a92cb0886f29671607f` (Rust `main`). Features: LOG-01. Redesign ticket: tracked privately.

Product rule: `guides/devtools.md`, "Log minimap", promises: "The log minimap shows output from every service as it arrives. Hover it to read the latest lines, with service names in a column and terminal colors kept readable in both themes." The devtools contract adds that "The injected devtools UI must remain visually isolated from the host page" (`packages/devhost-ui/src/devtools/AGENTS.md`). So a strip at the window's edge that shows every service's output as it arrives, and a hover peek at the latest lines, are intended and stay (`SURF-13`); a log surface that pushes, resizes or restyles the app's layout is not allowed (`SURF-5`). The gap is everything after the peek: lines cannot be kept open, selected, copied, read in full, narrowed to one service or reached by keyboard; the strip reaches only the lines that fit the window; stderr reads as failure; and the light theme loses the service's colors, which the docs promise to keep, so `source-and-minimap-06` is a code defect against that sentence, not a docs defect. Using devhost from another environment is a primary use case (`HOLD-8`): the logs socket sits behind `O36`'s check (decided 2026-11-25), so a browser signed in with the shared devhost passkey or paired with `devhost pair` reads logs exactly as this machine does, and any other browser gets the dock's unauthenticated state and no lines.

## Target experience

The person keeps working in the page; the strip on the right edge maps every line the page holds, with errors and warnings told apart by shape and color and every other line, stdout or stderr, drawn quietly. Hovering it peeks at the 15 lines under the pointer, in the service's own colors in both themes, and holds those rows still with "Held while you point · 7 new lines" while output keeps arriving. A click on the strip, **Logs** on the bar or Alt+Ctrl+O pins the log panel beside the page, at the width the person left it on any hostname of the stack, with focus in its find field: every line the page holds, each with its time and its source, wrapped within the panel, selectable and copyable, filtered by service with every service and devhost's own lines shown by default ("All sources · 512 lines"), and following new output until the person scrolls up, when **Jump to Latest · 7 new lines** brings them back. A failure's **Logs**, on a Services row or a rejected reload's notice, opens the same panel filtered to that service at its exit, showing the same lines of that service the crash view shows below its pinned exit summary; when that service still needs the person, the panel's status line offers the action that can fix it. The panel names where older output lives, `devhost logs api` and the stack's log file, so the terminal stays the full record without being the only one. From a phone or laptop in another environment, the person signs in once with the shared passkey (or pairs with `devhost pair`) and reads the same panel. Reading changes nothing, so there is no Undo; the panel's width and open state are the person's layout, changed back the same way they were set. Benchmarks: Tilt's per-resource log pane filters by resource, keeps every line selectable and copyable and follows new output until scrolled; Docker Desktop's container logs add timestamps, search and copy per container; VS Code's minimap maps a whole document onto one strip, and its Output panel keeps the selection while lines arrive; Chrome DevTools' Console filters by source and level and tells warnings from errors by icon as well as color; GitHub's and VS Code's light terminal themes map ANSI colors through a palette made for a light background instead of correcting a dark one; Slack's "Jump to latest" names how much arrived while the reader was away. Competitor behavior is evidence here, never a requirement.

## Target steps

Today: 5 steps that end in the terminal: notice the strip, hover its 12 px edge, move along it 15 rows at a time hunting for one service's line, read a row that cannot be selected, copied or read past the window's edge, then go back to the terminal to copy it. Proposed: 3 steps: open the log panel (click the strip, **Logs** or Alt+Ctrl+O), choose the service, or arrive filtered from a failure's **Logs**, then read and copy the line. The glance stays 1 step: hover the strip.

1. **Glance at the strip (`/__devhost__/ws/logs`, read only).** The 12 px strip at the window's right edge maps every line the page holds onto its height, newest at the bottom: one 2 px mark with a 1 px gap per line while they fit, else each mark stands for several consecutive lines, drawn from the most severe line it holds, so the oldest line the page holds is always at the top and reachable (`SURF-13`, `source-and-minimap-04`). Marks tell severity from the line, never from its stream: a line devhost reads as an error (a level word ERROR, FATAL or PANIC, or a message that starts with "Error:" or "panic:", after any timestamp and the service's own "[name] " prefix, and devhost's own failure lines) spans the strip's full width in the danger color; a warning (WARN or WARNING) spans five sixths of it in the warning color; every other line, stdout or stderr, scales with its length up to two thirds of the width in the faint color, so only errors reach the full width and a service that logs to stderr by default (Go's `log`, Python's `logging`) draws a quiet strip (`ACT-13`, `source-and-minimap-05`). devhost's own lines (step 4) are drawn in the toolbar's accent color, never a service's look (`source-and-minimap-11`). The strip shows the same lines as the log panel's source filter (step 4), read from one selector, so filtering the panel to api turns the strip into api's overview; the find text narrows only the panel's lines, never the strip (`SEL-1`, `SEL-10`, `source-and-minimap-04`).
   - Defaults: on, unless the manifest sets `[devtools.minimap].enabled = false` (branch "Turning the minimap off"); the strip appears once the page holds a line, as today, while **Logs** on the bar is there from the first page load (step 3).
   - The strip takes the page's pointer input only where it draws, its 12 px, and never widens on hover any more, so it never covers the dock's right end ("Hol" for **Hold Reloads** today) or any other devhost surface; the dock and docked chat keep shifting inward by its width (`SURF-1`, `SURF-5`, `SURF-11`; `surfaces-01`, `surfaces-10`, `source-and-minimap-07`).
   - The strip stays `aria-hidden`, because everything it offers, the peek included, is in the log panel, which **Logs** and Alt+Ctrl+O reach from the keyboard and which serves as its 24-pixel equivalent target (WCAG 2.5.8) (`KEY-12`, `SURF-9`).
2. **Peek by hovering (read only).** Hovering the strip opens the peek to its left: at most 15 rows around the hovered mark, each with the service column, devhost's own lines labelled "devhost" in the accent color, and the line in the service's own colors (step 5), one line per row, cut at the peek's edge, because the peek is a glance and the panel is where lines are read (`SURF-13`). The peek holds its own rows still while the pointer is on the strip or the peek, and its header says what arrived meanwhile, "Held while you point · 7 new lines", while the strip, the log panel and the crash view keep receiving every line; leaving the strip and the peek applies them (`source-and-minimap-07`). Its header also names the filter when one is active, "api · 14 of 512 lines", and the scale when marks stand for several lines, "512 lines · 1 mark = 2 lines" (`WORD-5`; `language-14`).
   - The peek is hoverable and dismissible (WCAG 1.4.13): the pointer can move from the strip onto it without closing it, and Escape hides it until the pointer leaves the strip and comes back, while the key still reaches the page, which keeps its own Escape (`KEY-5`, `KEY-8`; `keyboard-07`). A click on the strip or on a peek row opens the log panel scrolled to that line, highlighted (step 3).
   - The peek is placed beside the hovered marks and kept clear of the dock, the notice stack, docked chat and the open log panel, in either corner placement, so no devhost surface covers another's controls (`SURF-1`; `surfaces-01`, `source-and-minimap-07`).
3. **Open the pinned log panel (a click on the strip, **Logs** on the bar or Alt+Ctrl+O; read only, and one write to the person's layout record, `PUT /__devhost__/layout`, when it opens, closes or is resized, not disruptive).** The log panel is a docked panel along the right edge, between the strip and the dock, that overlays the page and never pushes, resizes or restyles it; the dock and docked chat shift inward by the panel's width as they do for the strip (`SURF-5`, `SD-4`; `surfaces-01`, `surfaces-12`). It opens at the width the person last left it, saved with its open state in the control server's layout record for the stack, never in browser storage, so it opens the same on every hostname of the stack and a page reload keeps it open (`SURF-7`; `surfaces-07`). Its header holds, in order: the source filter (step 4), the find field, the count line ("All sources · 512 lines"), **Copy 512 Lines** (step 5) and **Close**; below them the status line, then the lines, then **Jump to Latest** when it shows (step 6), and its last line names where older output lives (step 5).
   - Defaults: 480 px wide, or 40% of the window when that is narrower, never narrower than 320 px; every service and devhost's own lines shown; following new output; focus in the find field when it opens (`KEY-6`, `SEL-3`).
   - Keys: Alt+Ctrl+O (`logs`, configurable, hinted ⌃⌥O on macOS and Ctrl+Alt+O elsewhere from the binding's one definition, with `aria-keyshortcuts` on **Logs**) opens the panel with focus in its find field; pressed while the panel is open it closes it: from inside the panel focus returns per `KEY-6`, from the page focus stays where it is, so a page element the panel covers can be revealed without moving focus (WCAG 2.4.11) (`KEY-2`, `KEY-12`, `KEY-13`, `BAR-4`; `keyboard-02`, `keyboard-14`, `keyboard-15`). Escape anywhere in the panel closes it and returns focus to the control that opened it (**Logs** on the bar, or a rejected reload's **Logs**, whose notice stays), or, when a Services row's **Logs** opened it, to the **Services** trigger on the bar, because the Services panel, a toolbar panel, closed when focus moved into the log panel (`KEY-7`); when the strip's click opened it, focus returns to the page element that had focus before (`KEY-5`, `KEY-6`, `KEY-8`; `keyboard-07`). **Close** does the same. As a docked panel (`SD-4`, `SURF-5`) it stays open when focus leaves it, as `KEY-7` says of docked chat, and it keeps `KEY-3`'s DOM and Tab order (notice stack, bar, pinned log panel, docked chat): Tab past its last control moves on to docked chat's composer when chat is docked, else out of devhost, and a click on the page leaves it open (`KEY-7`, `KEY-3`). 2.11's rows for the log panel apply: the resize separator's arrow keys, Home and End resize it (this flow's steps: 16 px per arrow, Home and End to the narrowest and widest, a click stepping through 320, 480 and 640 px); Escape in the panel closes it with focus returned per `KEY-6`; and Escape while the minimap's peek shows closes the peek, focus staying where it is, while the key still reaches the page (`KEY-12`). Alt+Ctrl+B's cycle reaches it after the bar and before docked chat (`KEY-3`). The Tab stops follow the visual order: the resize separator, the source filter, the find field, **Copy**, **Close**, the status line's action when it shows (step 8), the lines element, **Jump to Latest** when it shows, and the two **Copy** buttons of the older-output line.
   - The resize handle on the panel's left edge is a focusable separator (`role="separator"`, `aria-orientation="vertical"`, `aria-valuenow` the width in CSS pixels, `aria-valuemin` 320 and `aria-valuemax` the widest the window allows), labelled "Resize log panel": ArrowLeft and ArrowRight widen and narrow it by 16 px, Home and End jump to the narrowest and widest, a click steps it through 320, 480 and 640 px, and a drag resizes it; each width is saved to the layout record (`SURF-13`, `KEY-12`, WCAG 2.1.1 and 2.5.7). The widest width leaves at least 240 px of the page visible.
   - The panel is a region named by its filter ("Logs: all sources", "Logs: api"); opening it is not announced beyond focus landing in its find field, and it is never a live region (`SURF-13`, `FB-9`).
4. **Choose what to read (the source filter and the find field, read only).** The source filter is a picker (`KEY-10`): a button naming the current choice, "All sources", that opens a list with a find field that filters it as the person types and highlights the matched text, listing every managed service of the stack with its line count and its state word from the shared state indicator and words module ("api · 14 lines · exited", "worker · 485 lines · running", "admin · no lines · not started"), a service's count including devhost's lines about it, so each count is the number of lines its choice shows and the counts can add up to more than the page holds (api's 14 are its own 12 lines and devhost's 2 about it, "Restarted api · 2.6 s · from the terminal" and "api exited with code 1 at 14:02:31", which "devhost · 9 lines" counts too), the services the run left out included (`SEL-10`, `SEL-3`, `WORD-6`, `ACT-6`; `selection-03`, `selection-13`, `language-13`). After a separator it lists "devhost", devhost's own lines: each operation's outcome line from the stack's log ("Restarted api · 2.6 s · from the terminal"), devhost's failure lines about a service ("api exited with code 1 at 14:02:31"), and each chat conversation's lines, agent-chat's one line per turn outcome ("Chat · Claude Code (Haiku 4.5) in fix-nav · replied · changed 2 files · 29 s"), labelled "devhost · Fix the nav" with the conversation's title, which the page reads from chat's conversation list by the line's session ID; the stack's log and terminal name the conversation by agent, model and worktree and never by its title, because log lines never hold message text (`CC-40`) (`FB-7`, `WORD-1`, `WORD-4`; `language-05`, `language-06`). An external service is absent from the list, and the list says once "External services run outside devhost; their output is not here", because devhost captures none of it (`MENU-3`, `ACT-9`). A service a hot reload removed keeps its lines until they age out, listed last with "not in the manifest".
   - Choosing a service shows its own lines and devhost's lines about it, each devhost line labelled "devhost", so api's exit sits after api's last line; "devhost" shows only devhost's own lines; "All sources" shows everything in order. The count line names what shows out of what the page holds, "api · 14 of 512 lines", and the strip follows the same choice (step 1) (`WORD-5`, `SEL-1`; `language-14`).
   - The find field filters the shown lines as the person types, case-insensitively, with the matches highlighted and the count line updated, "3 of 14 lines match "ECONNREFUSED""; Enter does nothing more (`SEL-10`, `KEY-9`). No match reads "No line of api matches "ECONNREFUSED"" with **Clear Filter**, which clears the find field and keeps the source (`STATE-2`).
   - Defaults: "All sources"; the source and the find text stay for this tab across page reloads, and **Clear Filter** returns to "All sources" with an empty find field.
5. **Read, select and copy (read only).** The lines are one focusable scrolling element (`tabindex="0"`, named "Log lines") that is never a live region; each line shows its time to the second ("14:02:31", local, 24-hour), its source (the service's name, or "devhost" in the accent color with the conversation's title for a chat line), then its text, wrapped within the panel without horizontal scrolling, its own ANSI colors and styles rendered and never printed as escapes (`SURF-13`, `SURF-9`, `WORD-8`; `language-09`, `language-18`, `source-and-minimap-07`). A line over 2,000 characters folds to its first and last 300 characters with **Show All 50,138 Characters** and **Copy**, as the shared recovery view folds it, because both render lines with one renderer (`SURF-9`, `SURF-14`). A stderr line carries a thin tick in the gutter, with "stderr" as its text for assistive technology, and only an error line (step 1's test) takes the danger bar and tint; a warning takes the warning bar, the level word keeping the service's own color (`ACT-13`, `source-and-minimap-05`). The person selects text with the pointer and copies it with the browser's own copy; the arrow keys, Page Up, Page Down, Home and End scroll the focused lines element, as the document's standard keys do. **Copy 14 Lines** copies the lines the filter shows as plain text, one per line with its time and source, a folded line in full, and without color codes, and the announcer says "Copied 14 lines" once (`FB-9`, `WORD-2`).
   - ANSI colors map through one palette per theme, the 16 standard and bright colors defined for the light and the dark background in `packages/design` (the light one in the hues of GitHub's light terminal palette: green, amber, red, blue, magenta, cyan); the 4.5:1 check stays as the final guard, and when it must adjust a color it lowers or raises its lightness in OKLCH with its hue kept, and never turns a colored fragment black or white unless the service asked for black or white (`source-and-minimap-06`). The peek, the panel and the shared recovery view (the crash view and the recovery page) render lines with this one renderer (`SURF-14`).
   - The panel's last line names where older output lives, as text with **Copy** beside each: "Older output: devhost logs api · ~/.local/state/devhost/logs/stack-3f9a….log", the command naming the filtered service and the file the stack's per-manifest log (`FB-11`, `SURF-15`; `surfaces-15`). When the server dropped older lines of a service, the panel says so at the top of that service's lines: "Older lines of worker were dropped; devhost logs worker has them".
6. **Follow new output, or hold the place (read only).** While the lines element is scrolled to its end, new lines appear at the bottom and it stays at the end. When the person scrolls up, it holds its place by line, whatever arrives or ages out, and **Jump to Latest · 7 new lines** appears below the lines, counting the lines that arrived since; pressing it scrolls to the end and follows again (`SURF-13`, `WORD-2`). New lines are never announced and the count is not a live region (`FB-9`). The panel renders at most once per animation frame, so a fast logger never freezes the page; that every line arrives is the log client's append fix, which startup-and-recovery ships (`startup-recovery-03`, `stack-scale-02`).
7. **Arrive from a failure (a failure's **Logs**, read only).** **Logs** on a service's row in the Services panel opens the log panel filtered to that service at its newest line; on a failed or exited service's row, and in a rejected reload's notice, it opens the panel filtered to that service, scrolled so its exit line, "api exited with code 1 at 14:02:31", is the last line in view with the service's last lines above it, the exit line highlighted; focus goes to the find field, as on every open (`SURF-13`, `MENU-5`, `KEY-6`; `feedback-08`). The crash view and the panel show that service's lines from one log store, the same lines in the same order, and render them with the one renderer of step 5 (`SURF-14`; `surfaces-02`). The crash view, startup-and-recovery's shared recovery view, opens its log at the end with the exit summary pinned above it, "api exited with code 1 at 14:02:31 · ERROR payment failed: Error: connect ECONNREFUSED postgres:5432", and shows a repeated identical block once with its count; the panel shows the same exit as devhost's line in its place among the lines (`SURF-13`, `FAIL-3`, `FAIL-4`). The lines a failure keeps (per service, frozen at the exit) are startup-and-recovery's backend; a rejected reload's notice keeping the crashed service's lines is manifest-hot-reload's (`startup-recovery-03`, `journey-crash-to-fix-03`).
8. **Act on a service that needs the person (a hand-off to the owning operation, one write).** While the filter shows one service whose state needs the person, the panel's status line states it in the shared state words with its cause and offers `MENU-5`'s lead action for that cause, the same control with the same request, reasons and outcome as the Services row's: "api exited with code 1 at 14:02:31" with **Restart api** (`POST /__devhost__/restart-service`, services-panel's operation); "admin is not started · left out of this run" with **Start admin** (`POST /__devhost__/start-service`); a service of a blocked repository with the blocked sentence, "The worktree ~/acme/.worktrees/fix-nav was deleted; its services still run from it", and a link that opens the Worktrees panel at **Return to Configured Worktree** (worktree-switch's) and **Check Again** (`MENU-5`, `MENU-6`, `HOLD-4`, `ACT-2`, `WORD-11`). A starting service reads "api is starting · health check 4 s of 60 s" and offers nothing, as `MENU-5` says (`ACT-4`, `STATE-3`). A pressed **Restart api** stays in place as the busy control with focus, "Restarting api…", and its outcome replaces it in the status line, "Restarted api · 2.6 s", taking focus on its sentence (`FB-2`, `KEY-11`, `FB-9`). It is the only write the panel starts; its blocking states are the operation's own: unavailable with "Lost connection to devhost; retrying" while the stream is lost, queued behind another per-service operation or a reload, unavailable with "Restart Stack is running · 2 of 3 started" while a stack-wide operation runs, and available with the reach line "uses the accepted manifest; devhost.toml line 12 is not applied" while a manifest edit is rejected (`HOLD-1`, `HOLD-2`, `HOLD-3`, `HOLD-6`, `SEL-7`). A browser in another environment that signed in with the shared passkey or paired restarts and starts from here exactly as this machine does; one that has not never gets the panel (branch "A page opened from another environment") (`HOLD-7`, `O36`, `O37`). `MENU-1` lists the pinned log panel's status line among a service's entry points, beside its Services row, the crash view and the recovery page, each handing off to the operation the Services panel owns.

Branches and edge states:

- **The keyboard path.** Alt+Ctrl+O opens the panel with focus in the find field; Shift+Tab reaches the source filter, whose Enter, Space or ArrowDown opens its list with focus in the list's find field, ArrowUp and ArrowDown move the highlight, Enter picks and Escape closes the list back to its button (`KEY-10`); Tab from the find field reaches **Copy**, **Close**, the status line's action when it shows, the lines element, where the arrows and Page Up, Page Down, Home and End scroll, **Jump to Latest** and the older-output line's **Copy** buttons; Escape closes the panel. Alt+Ctrl+B cycles the bar, the open panel, then docked chat's composer and back (`KEY-3`, `KEY-12`).
- **Nothing printed yet, or a service starting.** Before any line, the strip is absent and the panel's lines area reads "No output from any service since Acme Shop started at 14:02"; filtered to a service with no lines, "No output from api since it started at 14:02"; to a starting service, "api is starting · health check 4 s of 60 s · no output yet", never empty or failed; to a service left out of the run, "admin is not started · left out of this run" (`STATE-2`, `STATE-3`, `ACT-4`; `language-15`). While the first snapshot loads, the panel's frame shows at once with "Loading log lines…" where the lines go (`STATE-1`).
- **The lost connection.** The lines keep their times and the panel keeps everything it held, drawn as it was, its count line saying how current it is, "All sources · 512 lines · up to 16:24:07", and the source filter drawing each service's last known state as unknown, never down, "api · 14 lines · unknown · last seen exited at 16:24" (`ACT-5`); its status line reads the one reason the backend sent, "Lost connection to devhost; retrying", or the stopped stack's sentence, "Acme Shop stopped at 16:24 by devhost stop. Start it with devhost start in ~/acme", and **Restart api** and **Start admin** are unavailable with that same sentence as their accessible description (`ACT-5`, `HOLD-1`, `HOLD-2`, `STATE-3`; `feedback-11`). The reading stays usable: scroll, filter, find, select and copy. A resize still applies on this page, and the layout write is not sent; the panel says "Not saved: devhost is not reachable from this page", a refusal worded as what it is, and never sends it again for that width (toolbar-shell's layout behavior, `WORD-7`). notices-and-feedback owns the lost-connection notice and **Retry Now**.
- **The connection returns.** The fresh snapshot replaces the held lines, the panel keeps the person's place by line where that line is still held, and when lines were sent and dropped while the page was away (the snapshot's first line follows the last held one by more than one), a marker line between them reads "37 lines arrived while this page was disconnected and are no longer kept; devhost logs has them". When the stack was replaced and its line numbers start again, the panel shows the new snapshot from its start (`ACT-5`, `STATE-4`).
- **A fast logger.** Under 2,000 lines a second the panel stays at 60 frames a second, rendering at most once per frame and only the rows in view; once the log client appends every line (startup-and-recovery's fix), the panel shows every line in order. A held place stays held while lines arrive and older ones age out (`stack-scale-02`).
- **A service named chat, or devhost.** devhost's own lines carry their own source, never a service's name, so a stack's own service named `chat` shows only its output in the panel, the strip, its crash view and its failure page's Recent logs, and devhost's chat lines show under "devhost" with the conversation's title; a service named `devhost` is listed with the services, and devhost's own lines after the separator in the accent color (`source-and-minimap-11`). No service name is reserved, so no manifest stops loading.
- **The crash view or the Configuration window is open.** Both are modal: the open log panel stays visible and inert behind them, keeps receiving lines, and is live again with its place kept when they close or the crash view is minimized; the crash view shows the same lines itself (`SURF-3`, `SURF-4`, `KEY-7`; `surfaces-02`).
- **The page's own modal dialog.** The open panel stays visible and inert with the paused bar, keeps receiving lines, and is live again when the dialog closes; the reason is the one line in the notice stack that moves into the dialog, "Paused while this page's dialog is open"; the strip and the peek take no pointer input while the dialog is open; Alt+Ctrl+O, which acts while the page's modal is open, reports in that moved notice stack "The log panel is paused while this page's dialog is open" instead of doing nothing silently (`SURF-11`, `MENU-3`, `KEY-1`; `surfaces-10`).
- **A page element under the panel takes focus.** Alt+Ctrl+O hides the panel without moving focus, and pressed again reopens it with focus in it, because the panel never steps aside on its own (`SURF-9`, `KEY-12`, WCAG 2.4.11).
- **A narrow window.** At 900x900 the panel opens 360 px wide and at 1280x800 under 200% zoom (640x400 CSS pixels) 320 px wide, its header wrapping (the source filter and find field, then the count line, then **Copy** and **Close** together) with every control in view and only the lines scrolling; the dock's fit sequence (toolbar-shell's) takes the narrower room beside it. Below the acceptance viewports the panel takes the window's width less 16 px on each side (`SURF-9`, `O8`; `surfaces-09`). At 1600x1000, 1280x800 and 900x900 docked chat sits inward of the panel. Below 900 CSS pixels wide, as at 640x400, where `O8` gives docked chat the window's width less 16 px, the two never sit side by side: the one opened earlier folds to its bar control, **Chat** keeping the turn's state and **Logs** its count of new lines ("Logs · 12 new"), and returns as it was, with its draft, scroll and filter, when the later one closes. Folding is not closing: docked chat still closes only by its own **Close**, and the panel by **Close**, Escape or Alt+Ctrl+O (`SURF-5`, `KEY-6`, `O8`).
- **Turning the minimap off.** `[devtools.minimap].enabled = false`, saved in the manifest, removes the strip and its peek from open pages and nothing else: the log panel, **Logs** on the bar, Alt+Ctrl+O and every failure's **Logs** stay, and the crash view keeps its lines; the setting's description in `devhost.example.toml` and the docs say exactly that, and the reload's outcome line names it: "Applied devhost.toml: turned off the log minimap strip · the log panel stays on Logs · nothing restarted" (`SEL-11`; `selection-14`). `[devtools].enabled = false` turns off the whole toolbar, the log panel included (toolbar-shell's master switch).

## Recommended priority

**P2.** The realistic trigger is ordinary: any stack with one chatty service (a worker, a watcher, a poller) buries every other service's lines within minutes, and every service that logs to stderr by default paints the strip red, so a person who wants the one line that explains a failure holds the pointer still on a 12 px edge, reads 15 rows at a time and retypes what they see, or goes back to the terminal (`source-and-minimap-07`, `source-and-minimap-04`, `source-and-minimap-05`); light-theme users lose every color their tools print (`source-and-minimap-06`), and a stack with its own service named `chat` misreads agent lines as its output exactly when that service fails (`source-and-minimap-11`, P3). Reading logs writes nothing and loses no data, and the terminal keeps the full record, so the cost is time and trust, not work; that ranks it below services-panel and startup-and-recovery (P1), whose restarts and failure pages it reads beside, and the lines a crash needs most are startup-and-recovery's per-service retention, a P1 this flow does not own (`startup-recovery-03`). Ship first the UI-only slice that needs no backend: the per-theme ANSI palette, severity from the line, the strip that never widens over the dock, the hoverable peek holding its own rows with its count, and the strip mapping every held line. Then ship the log panel on today's stream: the panel with **Logs** and Alt+Ctrl+O once toolbar-shell's binding registry and bar land, the source filter, find, wrapping, selection, **Copy**, **Jump to Latest**, the separator, focus and the failure's **Logs** at the exit. Then the backend slice: times and sources on log entries with the recovery view's lines selecting service lines, devhost's own retention, the snapshot's log file, the layout record's `logs` field and the minimap switch's narrower reach, so no release shows devhost's lines as a source before they carry their own. Per-service retention and the append fix land with startup-and-recovery and change nothing in the panel but how far back it reaches. Seams, with no blocker beyond its dependency on the foundation toolbar-shell, which ships first or with the slice that needs it: toolbar-shell provides the bar's **Logs** item, the binding registry, Alt+Ctrl+B's cycle, the edge order and the layout route this panel extends; startup-and-recovery provides per-service retention, the append fix and the crash view that shows the same lines from this flow's store and renderer; services-panel's row **Logs** opens the panel, and its restart and start operations, shared state indicator and state words are what the status line reuses; notices-and-feedback builds the operation outcome lines the panel shows under devhost and owns the lost-connection notice and the announcer; agent-chat words the chat activity lines and their conversation titles, and builds `O36`'s check, which gates the logs socket (Backend 8) and draws the dock's unauthenticated state with toolbar-shell; manifest-hot-reload's rejection notice offers **Logs**; stack-run-stop provides `devhost logs` and the per-manifest log every stack writes; config-editor's window keeps no log viewer of its own, so output is read here after it closes.
