Read service logs in the page

TLDR: The strip at the window's edge maps every line the page holds, telling errors and warnings apart, and its hover peek holds still, while a click, Logs or Alt+Ctrl+O pins a log panel where each line has its time and source and can be filtered by service, found, selected, copied and followed, opened straight at a failure's exit with the action that fixes it.

Low-fidelity wireframe. Layout, names and numbers are illustrative; the redesign ticket's acceptance criteria are authoritative.

1. Glance at the strip

The 12 px strip still sits at the window's right edge, still shows every service's output as it arrives, still appears once the page holds a line, and the toolbar still shifts inward by its width, as today. What changes is what it maps and how it marks a line. On by default; [devtools.minimap].enabled = false turns it off. It stays aria-hidden, because everything it offers, the peek included, is in the log panel (step 3), which Logs and Alt+Ctrl+O reach from the keyboard and which serves as its 24-pixel equivalent target (WCAG 2.5.8) (KEY-12, SURF-9).

  1. 1 The strip maps every line the page holds onto its height, newest at the bottom: one 2 px mark with a 1 px gap per line while they fit, else each mark stands for several consecutive lines and is drawn from the most severe of them, so the oldest held line is always at the top and reachable; today only the newest lines that fit are drawn, about 267 of 512 at 800 px (SURF-13).
  2. 2 Severity comes from the line, never from its stream: a line devhost reads as an error (the level word ERROR, FATAL or PANIC, or a message starting "Error:" or "panic:", after any timestamp and the service's own "[name] " prefix, and devhost's own failure lines, such as "api exited with code 1 at 14:02:31") spans the full width in the danger color (ACT-13).
  3. 3 A warning (WARN or WARNING) spans five sixths of the width in the warning color; every other line, stdout or stderr, scales with its length up to two thirds of the width in the faint color, so only errors reach the full width, and a service that logs to stderr by default (Go's log, Python's logging) draws a quiet strip, never a red wall (ACT-13).
  4. 4 devhost's own lines, such as an operation's outcome or a chat conversation's activity (step 4), are drawn in the toolbar's accent color, never with a service's look. With the log panel filtered to one service, the strip draws the same lines as the filter, from one selector (step 4) (SEL-1, SEL-10).
  5. 5 The strip takes the page's pointer input only where it draws, its 12 px, and no longer widens to 96 px on hover, so it never covers the dock's right end or any other devhost surface; the dock and docked chat keep shifting inward by its width. Logs is on the bar from the first page load, before any line exists (SURF-1, SURF-5, SURF-11; surfaces-01, surfaces-10).
  6. 6 Saving [devtools.minimap].enabled = false removes the strip and its peek from open pages and nothing else: Logs, Alt+Ctrl+O, every failure's Logs and the crash view's lines stay, and the reload's outcome line says so, in the stack's terminal and log and among the panel's devhost lines (FB-7). The setting's description in devhost.example.toml and the docs name exactly what it removes; [devtools].enabled = false still turns off the whole toolbar, the log panel included (toolbar-shell's master switch) (SEL-11; selection-14).

2. Peek by hovering

Hovering the strip still opens a preview of at most 15 rows with a service column, without the service's own "[name] " prefix and with its terminal colors kept, as today. What changes is that the peek holds only its own rows, can be reached by the pointer, closes with Escape, and leads into the log panel. Reading changes nothing here; the peek is a glance, and lines are read, selected and copied in the log panel (step 3).

  1. 1 Hovering the strip opens the peek to its left at the hovered marks: at most 15 rows around them, each with the service column, devhost's own lines labelled "devhost" in the accent color, and the line in the service's own colors in both themes (step 5), one line per row, cut at the peek's edge (SURF-13).
  2. 2 The header names the scale when marks stand for several lines, "512 lines · 1 mark = 2 lines", and the filter when one is active, "api · 14 of 512 lines". While the pointer is on the strip or the peek, the peek's rows hold still and its header counts what arrived, "Held while you point · 7 new lines"; the strip, the log panel and the crash view keep receiving every line, and leaving the strip and the peek applies the waiting lines. Today hovering pauses the whole stream for every reader, with no cue (WORD-5; language-14).
  3. 3 Rows mark severity as the panel's lines do (step 5): the ERROR row takes the danger bar and tint, the WARN row has no danger tint and keeps its level word in the service's own color, and both carry the stderr tick; the last worker row, a plain stderr line from Go's log, keeps only its tick and reads quietly (ACT-13).
  4. 4 The peek is hoverable: the pointer moves from the strip onto it, here onto a worker row, without closing it, as WCAG 1.4.13 asks; today it ignores the pointer and closes as the pointer approaches. A click on the strip or on a peek row opens the log panel scrolled to that line, highlighted (step 3) (SURF-13).
  5. 5 The peek is placed beside the hovered marks and kept clear of the dock, the notice stack, docked chat and an open log panel, in either corner placement, so no devhost surface covers another's controls (SURF-1; surfaces-01).
  6. 6 Escape hides the peek until the pointer leaves the strip and comes back, while the key still reaches the page, which keeps its own Escape (KEY-5, KEY-8; keyboard-07).

3. Open the pinned log panel

New: a pinned log panel for reading, docked along the right edge between the strip and the dock. Three ways open it: a click on the strip (scrolled to the clicked line, highlighted), Logs on the bar, and Alt+Ctrl+O; a failure's Logs opens it filtered (step 7). It is a region named by its filter ("Logs: all sources", "Logs: api") and never a live region (FB-9). Keys: Alt+Ctrl+O opens and closes it, Escape or Close closes it, and as a docked panel it stays open when focus leaves it (callout 3).

  1. 1 Logs on the bar opens the panel, with its key in its tooltip and as aria-keyshortcuts, ⌃⌥O on macOS and Ctrl+Alt+O elsewhere, read from the logs binding's one definition (KEY-13, BAR-4; keyboard-02, keyboard-14, keyboard-15).
  2. 2 The header holds, in order: the source filter (step 4), the find field, the count line, Copy 512 Lines (step 5) and Close; below them the status line when a service needs the person (step 8), then the lines, Jump to Latest when it shows (step 6), and the line naming where older output lives (step 5). It opens with every service and devhost's own lines shown, following new output (SEL-3).
  3. 3 Focus lands in the find field on every open; opening is announced only by that. Alt+Ctrl+O (logs, configurable) pressed while the panel is open closes it, returning focus per KEY-6 from inside the panel and leaving it where it is on the page, so a page element the panel covers can be revealed without moving focus (WCAG 2.4.11); pressed again it reopens the panel with focus in it. Escape anywhere in the panel, or Close, returns focus to the control that opened it, or, after the strip's click, to the page element that had it. The panel keeps the dock's DOM and Tab order (notice stack, bar, log panel, docked chat): Tab past its last control moves on to docked chat's composer, else out of devhost, a click on the page leaves it open, and Alt+Ctrl+B reaches it after the bar. Tab stops follow the visual order: the resize separator, the source filter, the find field, Copy, Close, the status line's action (step 8), the lines, Jump to Latest and the older-output line's two Copy buttons (KEY-2, KEY-3, KEY-5, KEY-6, KEY-7, KEY-8, KEY-12, SURF-5).
  4. 4 The panel overlays the page and never pushes, resizes or restyles it; the dock and docked chat shift inward by its width. It opens at the width the person last left it, saved with its open state in the control server's layout record for the stack (PUT /__devhost__/layout), never in browser storage, so it opens the same on every hostname of the stack and a page reload keeps it open; the first time it opens 480 px wide, or 40% of the window when that is narrower, never under 320 px. The resize handle on its left edge is a focusable separator labelled "Resize log panel", its value the width in CSS pixels: ArrowLeft and ArrowRight widen and narrow it by 16 px, Home and End jump to the narrowest and the widest, a click steps through 320, 480 and 640 px, and a drag resizes it; the widest leaves at least 240 px of the page visible (SURF-5, SURF-7, SURF-13; surfaces-01, surfaces-07, surfaces-12).
  5. 5 At 1600×1000, from the window edge inward: the strip, the log panel, then the dock and docked chat, which shift inward by both widths and overlap neither. The 1280×800 and 900×900 frames draw docked chat inward of the panel the same way. Below 900 CSS pixels wide, as at 640×400, where O8 gives docked chat the window's width less 16 px, the two never sit side by side: the one opened earlier folds to its bar control, Chat keeping the turn's state and Logs its count of new lines, and returns as it was, with its draft, scroll and filter, when the later one closes. Folding is not closing: docked chat still closes only by its own Close (KEY-6, O8). The frames draw the dock at the bottom right; at the top right the same edge order holds (SURF-5, SURF-1; surfaces-01, surfaces-09).
  6. 6 At 900x900 the panel opens 360 px wide, and at 1280x800 under 200% zoom 320 px wide, its header wrapping (the source filter and find field, then the count line, then Copy and Close together) with every control in view and only the lines scrolling; the dock's fit sequence (toolbar-shell's) takes the narrower room beside it. Below the acceptance viewports it takes the window's width less 16 px on each side (SURF-9, O8; surfaces-09).
  7. 7 Before any line the strip is absent, but Logs is on the bar and the panel says since when it is empty, "No output from any service since Acme Shop started at 14:02"; while the first snapshot loads, its frame shows at once with "Loading log lines…" (STATE-1, STATE-2; language-15).
  8. 8 While the page's own modal dialog is open, the open panel stays visible and inert with the paused bar, keeps receiving lines, and is live again with its place kept when the dialog closes; the reason is the one line in the notice stack that moves into the dialog; the strip and the peek take no pointer input meanwhile; and Alt+Ctrl+O, which acts while the page's modal is open, reports "The log panel is paused while this page's dialog is open" there. The crash view and the Configuration window, both modal, leave the panel visible and inert behind them the same way (SURF-11, SURF-3, SURF-4, MENU-3, KEY-1; surfaces-10).
  9. 9 A browser in another environment that signed in with the shared devhost passkey or was paired with a devhost pair link gets the strip and the panel exactly as this machine does. Until it signs in or pairs, the dock shows its unauthenticated state, the sentence on the bar with Sign In with Passkey when the shared passkey is configured for the domain and how to get a devhost pair link: the strip is absent, Logs stays in place, unavailable with "Sign in with passkey or pair this browser to use devhost here" as its accessible description, Alt+Ctrl+O shows that sentence instead of opening the panel, the logs socket sends no line, and the app stays usable (HOLD-7, HOLD-8, MENU-3, O36).

4. Choose what to read

New: a source filter and a find field narrow what the panel shows, and the strip follows the same choice. Defaults: "All sources"; the chosen source and the find text stay for this tab across page reloads, and Clear Filter returns to "All sources" with an empty find field. Keys: the source filter is a picker; Enter, Space or ArrowDown on its button opens the list with focus in the list's find field, ArrowUp and ArrowDown move the highlight, Enter picks, and Escape closes the list back to its button; Enter in the panel's find field does nothing more, because it filters as the person types (KEY-10, KEY-9).

  1. 1 The source filter is a button naming the current choice, "All sources", that opens a list with a find field; typing filters the list and highlights the matched text (KEY-10, SEL-10).
  2. 2 The list holds every managed service of the stack with its line count and its state word from the shared state indicator and words module, the services the run left out included: "api · 14 lines · exited", "admin · no lines · not started"; a service's count includes devhost's lines about it, so each count is the number of lines its choice shows and the counts can add up to more than the page holds: api's 14 are its own 12 lines and devhost's 2 about it, which "devhost · 9 lines" counts too. A service a hot reload removed keeps its lines until they age out, listed last with "not in the manifest" (SEL-3, SEL-10, WORD-6, ACT-6; selection-03, selection-13, language-13).
  3. 3 After a separator, "devhost": devhost's own lines, each operation's outcome line from the stack's log ("Restarted api · 2.6 s · from the terminal"), devhost's failure lines about a service ("api exited with code 1 at 14:02:31"), and each chat conversation's lines labelled by the conversation's title, "devhost · Fix the nav". Choosing a service shows its own lines and devhost's lines about it, each labelled "devhost", so api's exit sits after api's last line; "devhost" shows only devhost's own lines (FB-7, WORD-1, WORD-4; language-05, language-06).
  4. 4 External services are not in the list, which says once why, because devhost captures none of their output (MENU-3, ACT-9).
  5. 5 The count line names what shows out of what the page holds, "api · 14 of 512 lines"; with find text, "3 of 14 lines match "ECONNREFUSED"", and Copy counts the lines shown (WORD-5; language-14).
  6. 6 The find field filters the shown lines as the person types, case-insensitively, with each match highlighted (SEL-10).
  7. 7 The strip draws the lines the source filter selects, read from the same selector: api's lines and devhost's lines about api, 14 in all, so each has its own 2 px mark; the find text narrows only the panel's lines, never the strip (SEL-1).
  8. 8 No match names what was searched, with Clear Filter; in the panel it clears the find field and keeps the source (STATE-2).
  9. 9 devhost's own lines carry their own source, never a service's name, so a stack's own service named chat shows only its own output in the panel, the strip, its crash view and its failure page's Recent logs, and devhost's chat lines show under "devhost" with the conversation's title; the line itself is agent-chat's one line per turn outcome, which names the agent, model and worktree and never the message. A service named devhost is listed with the services. No service name is reserved, so no manifest stops loading.

5. Read, select and copy

New: lines that can be read in full, selected and copied in the page. Reading writes nothing. Keys: the lines are one Tab stop; the arrow keys, Page Up, Page Down, Home and End scroll them, as the document's standard keys do; the browser's own copy copies a selection; Enter or Space on Copy copies the shown lines (KEY-12).

  1. 1 The lines are one focusable scrolling element named "Log lines" that is never a live region. Each line shows its time to the second, "14:02:31", local and 24-hour, its source, the service's name or "devhost" in the accent color, then its text, wrapped within the panel without horizontal scrolling; its own ANSI colors and styles are rendered, and escape codes are never printed (SURF-13, SURF-9, WORD-8; language-09, language-18).
  2. 2 A stderr line carries a thin tick in the gutter, with "stderr" as its text for assistive technology; only an error line takes the danger bar and tint; a warning takes the warning bar, its level word keeping the service's own color. Today every stderr line is a red row (ACT-13).
  3. 3 The person selects text with the pointer and copies it with the browser's own copy; here three lines are selected.
  4. 4 Copy 14 Lines copies the lines the filter shows as plain text, one per line with its time and source and without color codes, and the announcer says "Copied 14 lines" once (FB-9, WORD-2).
  5. 5 The panel's last line names where older output lives, as text with Copy beside each: the command for the filtered service and the stack's per-manifest log file, so the terminal stays the full record without being the only one (FB-11, SURF-15; surfaces-15).
  6. 6 ANSI colors map through one palette per theme, the 16 standard and bright colors for the light and the dark background in packages/design, the light one in the hues of GitHub's light terminal palette; the 4.5:1 check stays as the final guard and, when it must adjust a color, changes its lightness in OKLCH with its hue kept, never turning a colored fragment black or white unless the service asked for it. The peek, the panel and the shared recovery view (the crash view and the recovery page) render lines with this one renderer (SURF-14).
  7. 7 When the server dropped older lines of a service, the panel says so at the top of that service's lines.
  8. 8 A line over 2,000 characters folds to its first and last 300 characters with Show All 50,138 Characters and Copy, exactly as the crash view and the recovery page fold it, because all of them render lines with the one shared renderer; Copy 14 Lines copies a folded line in full (SURF-9, SURF-14).

6. Follow new output, or hold the place

New: the panel follows new output like a terminal, and keeps the person's place when they scroll up. Every service's output still reaches the page live, a snapshot when the page connects and then each line, and a dropped stream still retries after 1 s, backing off to 10 s, as today. Keys: Tab reaches Jump to Latest after the lines; Enter or Space presses it.

  1. 1 While the lines are scrolled to their end, new lines appear at the bottom and the view stays at the end. Once the person scrolls up, the view holds its place by line, whatever arrives or ages out (SURF-13).
  2. 2 Jump to Latest · 7 new lines appears below the lines, counting what arrived since; pressing it scrolls to the end and follows again. New lines are never announced and the count is not a live region (WORD-2, FB-9).

7. Arrive from a failure

A failure still shows in the Services panel, its notice and the crash view, and the crash view still lists the service's lines beside Restart, as today. What changes is that every failure's Logs lands in the log panel at the exit, and the panel and the crash view show the same lines. Keys: Logs is a Tab stop on the row and on the notice; focus goes to the panel's find field, as on every open. The Services panel, a toolbar panel, closes as focus leaves it, so Escape in the log panel returns focus to the Services trigger on the bar; from a rejected reload's notice, which stays, it returns to that notice's Logs (KEY-6, KEY-7, KEY-8).

  1. 1 Logs on a service's row in the Services panel opens the log panel filtered to that service at its newest line; on a failed or exited service's row, and in a rejected reload's notice, it opens the panel filtered to that service at its exit. These supplement the panel's own ways in: the strip, Logs on the bar and Alt+Ctrl+O (SURF-13, MENU-5; feedback-08).
  2. 2 The exit line, "api exited with code 1 at 14:02:31", is the last line in view, with the service's last lines above it, and it is highlighted, drawn here with the accent underline of the legend's "Line opened at" (SURF-13).
  3. 3 While the service still needs the person, the status line offers the action that can fix it (step 8).
  4. 4 The crash view and the panel show that service's lines from one log store, the same lines in the same order, rendered with the one renderer of step 5. The crash view, startup-and-recovery's shared recovery view, opens its log at the end with the exit summary pinned above it and shows a repeated identical block once with its count; the panel shows the same exit as devhost's line in its place among the lines (SURF-13, FAIL-3, FAIL-4). The lines a failure keeps, per service and frozen at the exit, are startup-and-recovery's backend; a rejected reload's notice keeping the crashed service's lines is manifest-hot-reload's (SURF-14; surfaces-02; startup-recovery-03, journey-crash-to-fix-03).

8. Act on a service that needs the person

New: the panel no longer leaves the person stranded after reading why a service failed. Restarting and starting stay services-panel's operations; the status line offers the same control, with the same request, reasons and outcome as the Services row's, and adds no write of its own. It is the only write the panel starts, and the panel's reading never waits for an operation. Chat that cannot take a message (HOLD-5) blocks nothing here: with chat off the panel reads, filters, finds and copies the same, and its action follows only its own operation's blocks. A browser in another environment that signed in with the shared passkey or paired restarts and starts from here exactly as this machine does; one that has not never gets the panel (step 3) (HOLD-7, O36, O37). MENU-1 lists the panel's status line among a service's entry points, beside its Services row, the crash view and the recovery page, each handing off to the operation the Services panel owns. Keys: the status line's action is the Tab stop after Close; Enter or Space presses it, and focus stays on it while it runs (KEY-11, FB-9).

  1. 1 While the filter shows one service whose state needs the person, the status line states it in the shared state words with its cause and offers MENU-5's lead action for that cause: for an exit, Restart api, which sends POST /__devhost__/restart-service with an operationId, exactly as the Services row's Restart does (MENU-5, MENU-6, ACT-2).
  2. 2 The pressed button stays in place as the busy control with focus, "Restarting api…", and its outcome replaces it in the status line, "Restarted api · 2.6 s", taking focus on its sentence (FB-2, KEY-11, FB-9).
  3. 3 A service the run left out offers Start admin (POST /__devhost__/start-service), as the Services panel's Start does (MENU-5, ACT-2).
  4. 4 A service of a blocked repository shows the blocked sentence, a link that opens the Worktrees panel at Return to Configured Worktree (worktree-switch's), and Check Again (HOLD-4, MENU-5, HOLD-1, WORD-11).
  5. 5 A starting service shows its health check's progress and offers nothing, as MENU-5 says for that cause (ACT-4, STATE-3).
  6. 6 Its blocking states are the operation's own, each one sentence that is the status line's text and every blocked control's accessible description: unavailable while the stream is lost or after the stack stopped, unavailable while a stack-wide operation runs, with that operation and its progress, queued behind another per-service operation or a reload with Cancel Restart, and available with its reach line while a manifest edit is rejected (HOLD-1, HOLD-2, HOLD-3, HOLD-6, SEL-7).
